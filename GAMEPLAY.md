## The story

You are a pilot. A wormhole accident throws your ship to the far side of the universe. The game
begins as you exit the wormhole — inside the cockpit, planet **MAGRATHEA** far ahead. You can steer,
but the planet's gravity has you. The cockpit fails, the camera falls, and you land with **fuel
empty and thrusters offline**. To find the way back you must mine the crust, rebuild the warp drive,
and burn for orbit.

Every escape throws you through a random deep-space event — storms, pirates, wormhole tremors,
derelict beacons — and damages the ship again. Each new planet is deeper, richer and more contested.
The way home is never the same twice.

## Launch sequence

Every page load plays the cockpit intro (about 9 seconds). It is skippable at any time with
**SKIP INTRO** or `Escape`; **⟲ REPLAY INTRO** is always available. The intro also preloads the
world and acts as the loading screen. `?intro=0` suppresses it (QA/share links).

After the intro you always land on the **main/tag screen**, even if you have a saved pilot, so you
can keep your callsign or enter a new game tag.

## Main screen

- **Enter tag** (2–12 characters: `A–Z`, `0–9`, `_`, `-`) and **JACK IN**.
- **NEW PILOT** clears this browser's saved pilot/tokens and lets you start over with a new tag.
- **ESCAPE MISSION** panel shows the current refit requirements (planet 1: 3 Uranium, 5 Silver,
  2 Gold, 1 Palladium).
- **REPLAY INTRO** reopens the cockpit cine.
- **Planet badge** shows the current world and levels (e.g. `MAGRATHEA · LEVELS 2`).

Each callsign is a persistent identity: its own colour, score, planet and ore inventory. Switching
to a different tag creates/restores a different pilot; the browser remembers one token per tag.

## Mining

- Click/tap a triangle: unclaimed → **claim**, rival-owned → **steal**, yours → **reinforce**.
  Every click adds charge to that cell.
- **Click threshold:**
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-mining -->
  Each planet has **one break threshold**: **4 clicks** on the first world, **8** on deeper worlds. At the threshold, that single triangle breaks into four smaller spherical triangles; children inherit the owner and start charging from 0.
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-mining -->
- **Contest lock:** after your own click on a cell, it turns **red** to show it is locked to you
  and only others can mine it. When another operator (human or robot) clicks it, the red clears and
  you can mine it again. A locked click shows `CELL LOCKED // CONTEST REQUIRED` once — no repeated
  `+2` popups.
- **Navigation:** a gold **NAV** chip shows the best nearby mining target (biome + deposit chance)
  with a pulsing beacon on the planet. Press the chip to fly the camera to it, or ↻ to cycle targets.
- **Robot operators:** a robot mines only the cell it stands on. Its route is generated by the
  server from the planet seed, so it walks, pauses, turns and mines without following any pilot;
  a target elsewhere is walked to before the beam can hit it.
- **Geography:** beneath the triangular grid is a high-detail procedural planet surface — ocean
  depth, coastlines, green lowlands, dry basins, mountain rock and polar ice. Cell edges and owner
  tints sit on top, so you always see the real geography behind your territory.
- **Hidden ore:** every mineable cell may hide a deposit. It is discovered on a **random successful
  click for that cell** — sometimes the first, sometimes a middle click, sometimes the last/breaking
  click. Discovery adds credits to your score, marks the cell, and updates the mission. Hidden
  deposits and their discovery click are never included in client data.
- **Ocean rule:** ocean/deep-ocean cells cannot be mined from the surface. The inspector shows
  `SUBMERGED // NO SURFACE DEPOSITS`. Coast cells can hold shallow placer deposits.

## Escape mission

Each planet requires a refit. Planet 1:

| Objective | Requirement |
|---|---|
| FUEL RODS | 3 × Uranium |
| HULL PLATES | 5 × Silver + 2 × Gold |
| WARP CORE | 1 × Palladium |

The landing briefing and `ESCAPE // SYSTEMS` HUD show progress live. When all three are complete,
the mission panel glows and a large centered **READY TO LEAVE** card appears with a pulsing
**TRY TO FLY AWAY** button. Press it to burn for orbit and start the interplanetary story; choose
KEEP MINING to dismiss the card and keep playing (the mission-panel escape button remains).

Later planets require more of the same ores. Requirements are derived from your **ore inventory for
the current planet**; boarding the next planet starts a fresh ore run (score carries).

## Planets, depth and progression

| Planet | Name | Levels | Cells when fully broken | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MAGRATHEA | **2** | 20 + 80 = **100** | tutorial planet, guaranteed refit ore, breaks at 4 clicks |
| 2 | TATOOINE | **3** | 20 + 80 + 320 = **420** | deeper grid, bigger refit, breaks at 8 |
| 3+ | ASGARD, KRYPTON… | 4 … 8 (cap) | progressively larger | increasingly deep/rich, infinite sequence |

A planet's "levels" count includes the base 20 faces: level 1 = 20, level 2 = the first 80 children,
and so on. The first planet therefore breaks **one generation** and stops: it cannot go deeper than
the second level.

- Each planet is its own **multiplayer scene**: its own players, robot(s), leaderboard, grid and
  resource deposits. You only see/fight people on your planet.
- **Emergency relief:**
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-relief -->
  If the refit makes no progress for two minutes, a relief ship crashes onto the planet and scatters the missing ore across nearby triangles. The deposits are marked with salvage rings and are mineable on the first successful click. The drop is **planet-wide**: the server places every human at the impact footprint on a distinct safe standing point inside a marked cell, every client sees the marked cells, the view turns toward the impact, and anyone can harvest the ore — it is a race. A pilot who joins while the drop is still live arrives already focused. Another ship can arrive after each two-minute window while parts are missing.
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-relief -->
- **Rare parts:**
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-rare -->
  Every refit includes at least one rare material; look for the **RARE** marker. Early planets are deliberately gentle: planets 1–2 need exactly one rare unit, planet 3 up to two, planet 4 up to three, then the deeper worlds ramp up.
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-rare -->
- **Quick Guide:**
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-quick-guide -->
  The first landing offers a one-screen answer to **what to mine**, **how to mine**, **where to dig** and **how to leave**; the same card is behind **GUIDE** and at `?guide=1` for sharing.
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-quick-guide -->
- **Planet character:** each world has its own major/secondary colour palette that tints the
  surface, atmosphere and HUD identity.
- **Planetary incidents:** when the last hidden deposit is mined, the exhausted planet ends in a
  random catastrophe — **Meteor Impact**, **Nuclear War**, **Solar Flare** or **Alien Invasion**.
  Each has a distinct full-screen visual effect, narration, shake and a story paragraph explaining
  the damage and the newly exposed resources, then resets/reseeds the crust with new resources.
  The Captain’s Log keeps the explanation after the overlay clears. Players, scores and inventories survive.
  The crust resets to its base **20 leaves** and the kind rotation is deterministic and durable per
  planet: four consecutive incidents are **Meteor Impact → Nuclear War → Solar Flare → Alien
  Invasion**, the cycle continues after a reload, and no kind can fire twice without the crust being
  re-armed by the reset. Each kind has one measurable visual signature built from the shared
  five-tetrahedron ship: meteor draws a relief ship among impact shards, nuclear opens on a
  full-frame flash with fire columns, solar draws a faceted sun with 48 corona rays, and alien draws
  two five-tetra ships with tractor beams. **DISMISS** or **Escape** closes the overlay and returns
  focus to the game; with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` one static poster frame replaces the
  animation and the screen never shakes. A development-only trigger
  (`NARIMATO_EVENT_TRIGGER=1` then `POST /api/action` with `{"type":"incident","kind":"solar"}`)
  fires a chosen kind without mining, and is refused with `403` when the variable is unset.
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-incidents -->
  - **METEOR IMPACT** — A rogue meteor punched through the surface. The old tunnels are gone, but the crater cracked open fresh ore veins and threw rare material across the ground. Get down there and salvage what the rock is hiding.
  - **NUCLEAR WAR** — Atomic fire swept the planet from horizon to horizon. The blast waves split the crust and exposed new materials. The survivors are already picking through the rubble.
  - **SOLAR FLARE** — The local star lashed out and cooked the daylight side. The magnetic storm peeled back rock and scattered rare elements across the new ground. The ore is close.
  - **ALIEN INVASION** — An alien fleet razed the planet from orbit. Their beams demolished the old surface and scattered new materials across the wreckage. Whatever they wanted, they left plenty behind for us.
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-incidents -->
- **Carried ore:** your full ore inventory (all materials, ore counts and total ore value) travels
  with you when you escape. It is available immediately on the next planet and counts toward that
  planet's refit, so stockpiling can pay off. The **CARGO // ALL MATERIALS** panel shows everything
  you have collected, including ore beyond the current requirements.
- **Random destinations:** an escape burn targets one of the next **five** planets at random, never
  backwards. Each destination has its own procedurally generated mission and multiplayer scene.
- **Interplanetary transit:** after ESCAPE, the ship burns out of orbit, a random story event hits,
  the hull takes damage with screen shake, and you land on the next planet. The cinematic is fully
  procedural (CSS/Canvas, no assets). On touchdown, a centered **landing briefing** lists every ore
  required to leave the new planet and your collected/required counts; press START MINING to dismiss.
- **Camera feel:** mouse drag follows the cursor directly — drag left, the planet rotates left,
  even when the planet is upside down. Release leaves the view where you left it. Arrow-key travel
  is deliberately inverse: press left and the planet rotates right. Wheel/pinch zoom glides into
  its target instead of snapping, so fine control at deep zoom is easier.

## Leaving / starting over

- **ABANDON** (desktop status bar, or the mobile dock) → confirmation dialog → returns to the
  main/tag screen. Your pilot and planet progress are saved; you can resume with the same tag.
- **NEW PILOT** on the main screen clears this browser's saved pilot tokens so you can enter a new
  tag and start a new mission from planet 1.
- If you simply close the page, your pilot survives server-side and can rejoin later.

## Narration, subtitles and the Field Manual

NARIMATO behaves like a streaming player: **VOICE** and **CC** are independent toggles in the top
status bar, and both remember your choice.

- **VOICE** plays prerecorded narration: the cockpit story, context tips, Field Manual sections,
  the random interplanetary story after every escape, emergency relief drops, each catastrophe,
  the Quick Guide and the Captain's Log. Narration is deliberately slow and plays **one sentence
  at a time**, with a short breath pause between sentences; CC follows the same sentence units.
- **CC** shows the same lines as subtitles. They appear immediately even if the browser blocks
  autoplay until your first tap/click. Speech clips are prefetched in the background (first line of
  each cue at idle, then the next sentence while the current one plays) so a slow connection does
  not leave a gap between sentences.
- **?** opens the **Field Manual**: the story, mining and contest rules, planets/escape, navigation,
  controls and audio. Each section has a **▶ PLAY** button. Under **NARRATOR** you can switch
  between **GEORGE** (open-source Kokoro, default), British **DANIEL** and US **SAMANTHA**; the
  choice is remembered.
- **Captain's Log:**
  <!-- story-copy:start gameplay-captain-log -->
  The log tells you in plain language what the ship still needs, for example "We're grounded on TATOOINE — STAR WARS. The drive still needs 2 Uranium and 1 Palladium. The crust can give it to us."
  <!-- story-copy:end gameplay-captain-log -->

- First-time hints are one-time and can be replayed from the manual. Story interplanetary narration
  extends the transit screen so the story is not cut off.

## Planet names

Every planet is a cultural reference — planet names are drawn from a curated
catalog spanning *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*, Star Wars, Marvel, DC,
Dune, Doctor Who, Star Trek, Alien, Avatar, Foundation, Transformers, classic
myth and more. The **landing briefing** and planet badge show the name and its
source (e.g. `MAGRATHEA // HITCHHIKER'S GUIDE`). Names cycle with a suffix after
the catalog is exhausted, so the infinite frontier never runs dry.

## Multiplayer

- Players are matched per planet. Robots are always present: one robot per loaded planet, with a
  new creative callsign/colour each spawn, playing for a random time, then leaving and respawning.
- Each active planet keeps **3 players online**: if one human is present, 2 robots join; two humans
  get 1 robot; three or more humans run bot-free. Replacements spawn immediately when someone
  leaves, and an excess robot steps aside when a new human arrives.
- On the crust, humans and robots appear at their **server-owned poses** and disappear after five
  seconds of silence — a closed tab leaves no ghost body. Robot movement is deterministic from the
  planet seed, so it cannot be steered by where a pilot stands.
- Pilots and robots are drawn as **3D bodies** — head, visor, torso, hips, arms and legs connected
  at the joints — with a walk cycle and a tool-arm lift while mining. Nearby figures keep full
  detail; distant ones switch to a simplified body and then a silhouette, so the crust stays
  readable with a crowd on it.
- **Mining beams are server events, not client effects.** A beam starts at the operator's pose and
  lands on the exact cell the server changed; it never fires from a client-side timer. If the
  target cell is out of view, an edge chevron points at it. Reduced motion freezes the walk and
  shows mining as a static marker.
- **Emergency supply drops are real server events.** The relief ship places one hidden deposit per
  missing refit part on nearby mineable cells and relocates every online human into the impact
  footprint, so both pilots can compete for the same salvage; the orbit camera focuses the impact.
- The robots deliberately contest charged human cells so solo play cannot speed-run the lock.
- The arcade leaderboard is per planet, all-time for that scene; planet carries your score forward
  to the next scene.

## Reports and sharing

**REPORT** summarizes rank, total score, session gains, clicks, breaks, claims, steals, faces held,
depth, ores mined, ore value and richest find. Download a 1200×630 PNG or share a self-contained
URL. Reports include the current planet and arcade context.

## Controls & accessibility

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Click/tap triangle | claim / steal / reinforce + charge |
| Drag | rotate |
| Wheel / pinch | zoom |
| Double-click | focus/drill |
| `L` | all face ids (debug) |
| WASD / arrows / touch pads | steer during intro |
| `Escape` | skip intro / close dialogs |
| `R` | reset camera |

- Intro and interlude respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Mobile/tablet layouts use larger touch targets, safe-area insets, drawers and a tap target sheet.
- Face ids are shown only on hover/tap and briefly after a click (`L` toggles all).

## Tips

- Break cells where other players/robots are active; contested cells unlock your own.
- Coastal and mountain cells are the best early ore bets; avoid deep ocean.
- Fill FUEL first — uranium appears in hills/mountains and is worth more score.
- The mission counts every future find; existing inventories persist forever.
- If a planet becomes mined out, the meteor reset is a chance to claim fresh, unowned ground.
